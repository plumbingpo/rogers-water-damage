Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Rogers

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Air From Below

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
