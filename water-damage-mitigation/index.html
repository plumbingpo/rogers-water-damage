Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Rogers crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. In fact there are two separate stages. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Rogers

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Rogers Restoration:

1

Stop the Water Source

We shut off the supply, cap the failed line, or tarp and board the opening where water is entering. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.
