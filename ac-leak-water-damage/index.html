AC Leak Water Damage Help in Rogers

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.
