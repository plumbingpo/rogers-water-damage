Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in Rogers, AR

Home / Blog / Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in Rogers, AR

A burst pipe is one of the most stressful plumbing emergencies a homeowner can experience. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. What you do in the first few minutes after finding the break often decides whether you face a simple repair or a full restoration, and it can keep thousands of dollars of damage off the bill. Below, Rogers Restoration lays out a step by step plan that homeowners in Rogers, AR can follow when it happens.

Step 1: Shut Off the Water Supply Immediately

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Every minute the water keeps flowing adds to the damage and the cost. Here's what to do: locate your main water shutoff valve. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Rogers houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. Rotating it clockwise, the old righty-tighty rule, cuts water to the whole home. If you can identify the specific pipe that burst and it has its own shutoff valve nearby, close that valve as well.

Your water company can cut the supply at the meter, so call them at once if the valve is missing or will not budge. Knowing where that valve lives ahead of time, not during a crisis, matters for exactly this reason. Go look for it today, then check that the handle turns without a fight. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

There is useful work to do until help shows up: first, carry anything valuable, plus paperwork and electronics, to a dry room. Lift furniture off wet carpet by placing aluminum foil or plastic blocks under legs. Remove area rugs from wet floors. Mop up accessible standing water with towels. Air needs to circulate, so leave cabinets and closets open. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker box, but only when that is safe and you are not standing in water.

Never walk through standing water near electrical outlets, appliances, or panels. When flooding is heavy and there is no safe route to the panel, do not try to shut the power off; let professionals evaluate it first. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Document Everything for Insurance

Before you begin cleaning up, document the damage thoroughly for your insurance claim. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. Rogers Restoration supplies detailed reports as well, and paired with your notes they strengthen your claim and your case for fair payment.

Preventing Burst Pipes in Rogers

Insulating bare pipes in crawl spaces, garages, attics, and other unheated areas is a big step Rogers homeowners can take, even though no measure prevents every burst. A slight drip from your faucets in a cold snap keeps the water in motion. For sinks on outside walls, leave the cabinet doors beneath them open so warm air gets to the plumbing. Keep an eye on water pressure, and if it runs above 80 PSI, have a pressure reducing valve put in. Braided stainless steel should take the place of worn supply lines, rubber washing machine hoses above all. Above all, make sure you can find your main shutoff long before an emergency.
