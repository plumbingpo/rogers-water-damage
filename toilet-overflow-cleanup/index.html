Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Rogers

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

Our Rogers crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Block the SpreadUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.

Insurance

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Flushed wipes, excess paper or a small toy caught in the trap.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Odor That Returns

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.
